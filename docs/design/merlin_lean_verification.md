# Lean verification of the Merlin compilation path outside the OOT

## 0. Summary

Merlin compiles a captured PyTorch model into a bare-metal ELF. One stage of that pipeline, the
target's out-of-tree (OOT) compiler, is generated by an agent and is out of scope here. Everything
else is Merlin's, and this design makes it **verifiable compilation**:

- Every Merlin-owned transformation is run as it is today, and its *exact* input and output IR
  (plus an untrusted *certificate* the pass emits) are handed to a **checker written in Lean 4**.
- Each checker carries a **machine-checked soundness theorem**: *if the checker accepts, the output
  program refines the input program* (or, for numerics-changing passes, *implements the declared
  numerical policy*), for **all inputs and all shapes the IR states**, under **every interpretation of
  floating-point operations**.
- Checks are bound to the bytes actually compiled, chained end to end, and composed by a Lean theorem
  into one statement per validation program.
- Accelerator calls are **holes with stated contracts**. The Merlin-side result is *assume-guarantee*:
  if every device symbol meets its contract, the host-side compilation is correct. Discharging those
  contracts is the post-submission OOT work. Today they are discharged empirically by L3 grading.

This is the *credible compilation* architecture: unverified passes, plus a verified checker on every
run. Rinard's Axon work measured it at roughly an order of magnitude less effort than verifying the
passes themselves ([arXiv 2605.08927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08927),
[arXiv 2605.01660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01660)). It fits Merlin because the passes are Python
and change often, while the checkers and their theorems are small and stable.

**What this design does not do** is prove LLVM, upstream MLIR bufferization, the C toolchain,
hardware, or floating-point *accuracy*. Those are listed in a trusted-computing-base ledger (§10).
The paper states them as assumptions; no checker reports them as verified.

## 1. Goal, claim, and non-negotiables

### 1.1 The claim we intend to publish

For each validation program `P` (the `phase1_gates.private_full_models` roster: `resnet50.model`,
`smolvla.{prefix_encode, flow_denoise, action_decode}`, `tiny_llama.{prefill, decode}`, int8
deployment), Merlin produces a chain of intermediate programs `S0 → S1 → … → Sn`:

- `S0` is the capture;
- `Sn` is the tensor program handed to upstream MLIR bufferization/LLVM lowering.

Every boundary `Si → Si+1` is either:

- **verified**: a Lean-checked certificate establishes its claim (§3, D7); or
- **assumed**: listed by name in the claim; or
- **trusted**: part of the TCB (§10).

The composed theorem (§5.10) states:

> For every input on which the source is defined, under every interpretation of floating-point
> operations satisfying the named laws, and assuming each accelerator call meets its stated contract,
> `Sn` refines `Policy(Sk)`, where `Sk` refines `S0`. Here `Policy` is the declared W8A8 numerical
> policy (§5.5) and `k` is the first policy-changing boundary.

The paper reports, per program, which boundaries are verified, assumed, and trusted. Separately it
reports the downstream *safety* checks (ABI binding, memory map), the semantics-fidelity evidence, and
the qualification results (seeded faults, historical bug replay).

### 1.2 Non-negotiables (no shortcuts)

These rules are enforced by tooling (§8.6), not by goodwill:

1. **No unproved Lean in a claim.** The following are build failures inside the verified namespaces:
   - `sorry`, `admit`, or a new `axiom` outside `Assumptions/`;
   - `native_decide`;
   - `@[implemented_by]`, `@[extern]`, `unsafe`, or `partial def`.

   `#print axioms` of every top-level theorem must be a subset of `{propext, Classical.choice,
   Quot.sound}` plus the named, reviewed entries of `Assumptions/`.
2. **Certificates are hints, never evidence.** A wrong certificate can only cause a rejection. Every
   fact a soundness proof needs (purity, use-def, side conditions, liveness, shapes) is recomputed by
   the checker from the IR.
3. **Unsupported is never verified.** An op, attribute, or pattern outside the semantics yields
   `abstracted` with a reason. A boundary is verified only if *every* changed site is accounted for.
4. **Denominators are fixed before checkers are built.** The stage inventory (WP-0) pre-registers which
   boundaries and sites exist on the paper path. Coverage is measured against it, so scope cannot
   shrink silently.
5. **Claim statements and specifications are frozen and reviewed.** Proofs may change freely. The
   statements of top-level theorems, the semantics of each op, and the policy specs change only through
   review (§11.4).
6. **Specs are written behind an information barrier.** A policy spec (W8A8, entry semantics) is written
   from the declared policy and PyTorch/TorchAO references by someone who has not read the implementing
   pass. Otherwise the spec copies the bug.
7. **Non-vacuity is tested.** Every checker must accept real artifacts from the paper pipeline. A
   checker that rejects everything fails qualification, as does a refinement against an undefined
   source (§4.5).
8. **Instrumentation is byte-neutral.** Turning certificate recording on must not change any emitted
   IR, object, or ELF (digest test).

## 3. Architectural decisions

Each decision records the alternatives and why we chose this one. They were made with the paper's
reputation as the binding constraint: we prefer a smaller claim that is airtight to a larger one with
a hole.

### D1. Verified checkers, not verified passes and not SMT alone

- **Chosen:** credible compilation. Passes stay in Python. A Lean checker with a soundness proof
  validates each actual run.
- **Rejected: rewriting the passes in Lean and proving them.** That is a new compiler. It is about 10×
  the effort (Axon's measurement), and the proofs would cover the Lean version, not the Python code
  that actually runs.
- **Rejected: extending the existing Z3 translation validation.** Its theorems hold *at one concrete
  shape*, solver cost grows with shape (the `verify/AGENT.md` measurements), and it encodes the
  programs directly rather than checking local certificates. We keep it as an independent cross-check
  (D12).
- **What Lean adds:**
  - theorems quantified over all shapes, widths and float models;
  - checking cost linear in program size, independent of tensor extents;
  - a small, reviewable statement surface.

### D2. Certifying passes emit hints, and the checker re-derives everything

A pass records, per rewrite site, the rule it applied and the rule's parameters (for example, "site
`%42` replaced by instance of `dequant_per_channel_to_generic` with axis 0"). The checker:

1. Re-parses both programs.
2. Recomputes the graph difference.
3. Requires every changed op to be covered by exactly one certificate entry.
4. Re-checks each rule's side conditions on the IR.
5. Matches the instance.

A missing, wrong or extra entry is a rejection. So certificate recording is untrusted, and a bug in
it cannot produce a false "verified". Several Merlin passes already expose rewrite callbacks
(`lower_quant_ext(on_rewrite=...)`, `carry_provenance`), so most need a recorder call rather than a
redesign.

### D3. Our own semantics in Lean 4 + Mathlib; Lean-MLIR as a reference, not a dependency

- **Lean-MLIR** ([opencompl/lean-mlir](https://github.com/opencompl/lean-mlir)) proves SSA peephole
  rewrites with intrinsically typed programs and bit-vector semantics. Its `denote_rewritePeepholeAt`
  theorem is the analog of our congruence theorem (§5.1).
- **We do not depend on it,** for three reasons, checked against its `main` at `b8c4a77`
  (2026-07-28):
  - It is pinned to `leanprover/lean4:nightly-2025-12-01` with a Mathlib `nightly-testing` revision.
    It cannot share a Lake workspace with a v4.35.0 project without a port.
  - Its framework (`LeanMLIR/`) ships one dialect (LLVM), a generic-syntax MLIR parser, and verified
    DCE/CSE/peephole rewriting. Tensor support is two small research projects
    (`SSA/Projects/Tensor1D`, `Tensor2D`, ~600 lines together). There is no `linalg`.
  - Intrinsic typing makes ingesting large external programs at run time harder.
- **What we take from it:**
  - **Prior art:** its verified DCE/CSE and peephole theorem are prior art for C1, and its generic
    parser is prior art for D9.
  - **Reuse:** where licensing permits (Apache-2.0), we copy integer and LLVM op definitions with
    attribution.
  - **Reference checkout:** `~/Projects/lean-mlir`.
- **Mathlib** provides algebra on `BitVec` and `Fin`-indexed sums: commutativity and associativity
  lemmas for reordering integer reductions, and permutation and big-operator reasoning. Its build cost
  is paid once via `lake exe cache get`.

### D4. Extrinsic AST, total denotation, refinement with poison and UB

- **IR representation.** Programs are an untyped AST decoded from JSON, plus a decidable typing check.
  The denotation is total. It returns an `Outcome` (`ub` or `ok` values), where values may contain
  `poison`.
- **Refinement** is the standard translation-validation relation (§4.5). It is reflexive and
  transitive (proved), so boundary results compose.
- **Why extrinsic:** it makes JSON ingestion trivial, keeps agents productive, and avoids dependent-type
  elaboration costs on thousand-op programs.

### D5. Floating point is parametric

Float values are bit patterns. Every float operation (`addf`, `mulf`, `divf`, `maximumf`, `roundeven`,
`exp`, `fptosi`, `sitofp`, …) is interpreted by a `FloatModel` *parameter* with **no laws by
default**. Theorems quantify over all models, so they hold for IEEE-754 binary32 on the target in
particular. Four consequences, all intended:

- **Exact-order equality.** A rewrite that reassociates, reorders reductions, or constant-folds floats
  *cannot* be proved this way, and must not be. On real hardware, such rewrites change bits.
- **Named laws when needed.** If a rule needs a genuine IEEE fact (for example, sign symmetry of
  `negf`), the fact is a field of a `FloatLaws` structure. The theorem takes it as a hypothesis, and
  the ledger lists it with its IEEE justification and target caveats (NaN payload rules differ across
  ISAs).
- **Concrete evaluation (C6).** Constant hoisting and prepacking compute float values at compile time.
  Those checkers instantiate the model with an *executable IEEE binary32/bf16 implementation*. The
  claim then holds for that instance, and the ledger records the fidelity assumption: the executable
  instance equals the target's IEEE behavior for the basic ops used.
  - Unary ops (`roundeven`, `fptosi`, `truncf`, `extf`) are tested **exhaustively over all 2³²
    inputs**. This is not a formality. Lean's `Float32.round` rounds half away from zero (2.5 → 3.0,
    checked on v4.35.0), while MLIR's `math.roundeven` rounds half to even (2.5 → 2.0). The instance
    must implement `roundeven` itself rather than reuse a similarly named library function.
  - Binary ops are tested by structured random and boundary sampling against the target ISA
    simulator.
  - Transcendentals are never concretely evaluated.
- **No error bounds.** Accuracy against FP32 PyTorch is measured, never proved, in this design.

### D6. Integers and index are exact `BitVec`

- `iN` is `BitVec N`, including MLIR's `nsw`/`nuw` (overflow produces poison) and immediate UB for
  division by zero or signed overflow in `divsi`.
- `index` is `BitVec indexBits`, instantiated with the width the lowering recorded
  (`index_lowering.index_bits`).
- Integer reductions may be reordered by proof: `BitVec` addition is a commutative group. This is what
  makes integer tiling, split-K, and W8A8 accumulation arguments exact.

### D7. A claim type for every boundary

The single most important honesty mechanism. Every boundary declares exactly one claim type. The
paper's wording is generated from it.

| Claim type | Meaning (Lean) | Typical passes |
|---|---|---|
| `equiv` | `after ⊑ before` and `before ⊑ after` on the domain | outlining, DCE, CSE, exact lowering |
| `refine` | `after ⊑ before` (may remove UB/poison, e.g. defining an accumulator) | zero-fill, DCE of UB-capable ops |
| `equiv_args π` | equivalence modulo an argument/result map `π` | quant-inner lift, entry-weight projection |
| `policy P` | `after ⊑ P(before)` for a reviewed spec transformer `P` | W8A8 quantization, integer nonlinears |
| `erasure` | `after` equals `before` with listed annotation attributes/ops removed, and those annotations are semantics-free by spec | `llvm.emit_c_interface`, `prov.*`, contract/schedule annotations |
| `partition` | a structural property (every computational op in exactly one group), no semantic change | compute groups |
| `contract_seam` | `after ⊑ before` under an environment where each new external symbol satisfies its stated contract | device routing |
| `safety` | a non-functional property (disjointness, bounds, binding) | memory map, argument table |

**Numerics-changing passes are never `equiv`.** The existing comment in `passes_quant_int.py` already
says the per-tensor `quantize_before_gather` mode "must not be reported as" bit-identical. This table
makes that a type error instead of a comment.

### D8. Accelerator calls are contract holes (assume-guarantee)

At the tensor level, device routing replaces a call to an outlined group function `@forward$kernel_i`
with a call to an external symbol `@dev_i`. Semantically, external symbols are interpreted by an
environment `Γ`. The Merlin-side theorem quantifies over every `Γ` with
`Contract(Γ, dev_i) := Γ dev_i = ⟦kernel_i⟧`.

Three Merlin obligations make the hole well-posed:

1. **C7a:** the seam rewrite itself.
2. **C7b:** the entry handed to the OOT denotes exactly `⟦kernel_i⟧`, under the declared layout
   restatement.
3. **C7c:** the host-side ABI bridge (shim, prepack) maps the tensor-level call onto the kernel's
   buffer-level contract.

The same `EntrySpec` Lean function is the specification the OOT must later be verified against, so
this is also the interface for the post-submission work.

### D9. IR enters Lean through MLIR's own parser, and the loop is closed by round-trip

- **Exporter.** `merlin-mlir-export` is a small C++ tool linked against the *same* MLIR the pipeline
  runs. It walks generic-form operations through the MLIR C++ API and emits JSON (schema
  `merlin.lean.ir.v1`). Attributes come out *typed*: affine maps as expression trees and dense elements
  as raw bytes with type and shape. There is no string re-parsing, which satisfies the repo's no-regex
  rule.
- **Why MLIR's parser:** the final stage is consumed by `mlir-opt`, so MLIR's reading of each stage is
  what reaches the binary.
- **Round trip.** Lean pretty-prints its decoded AST back to generic MLIR. `mlir-opt
  --mlir-print-op-generic --allow-unregistered-dialect` canonicalizes both that print and the original,
  and the results must be byte-identical. This validates JSON decoding and export per instance.
- **Unregistered dialects.** Merlin's xDSL-only dialects (`quant_ext`, `prov`, `contract`, `schedule`,
  `interface`) round-trip as unregistered generic ops.
- **Dual export.** Where a stage's *input* is not MLIR-parseable (the textual printer-quirk route, U16),
  a secondary exporter walks the xDSL in-memory module. The checker proves the two JSON graphs
  isomorphic modulo the declared spelling normalization.
- **Rejected:** parsing MLIR text in Lean (a large, fragile grammar) and trusting xDSL's parser alone
  (documented printer skew).
- **Fallback:** if building the C++ tool against the pinned LLVM proves impractical, rebuild that LLVM
  with `MLIR_ENABLE_BINDINGS_PYTHON=ON` and use the MLIR Python API. The local build has bindings off.
- **Amendment (2026-10-13): the pipeline's MLIR is the authoritative parser.** The paper path lowers
  with the MLIR bundled in the `torch_mlir` wheel (§2.1), not the llvm-project build.
  - The exporter therefore parses with that MLIR, through its Python bindings (`torch_mlir.ir`), in
    the same environment the pipeline uses. This needs no C++ build, and it guarantees the verified
    reading is the compiled reading.
  - Round trip uses that same MLIR's printer.
  - The llvm-project build is only a cross-check, and a disagreement between the two parsers is a
    finding.
  - Receipts record the wheel's version and bundled MLIR revision.
  - Caveat for the exporter: MLIR accepts inherent attributes inside the discardable dictionary, so
    a props/attrs mix-up can still round-trip byte-identically. The exporter must therefore register
    exactly the dialects the pipeline registers.

### D10. Receipts are byte-bound, chained, and composed in Lean

**Receipt contents.** A receipt (schema `merlin.lean.receipt.v1`, extending `verify/receipts.py`) binds:

- the before, after and certificate bytes;
- the exporter binary and its MLIR revision;
- the Lean toolchain and Mathlib revisions, and the checker executable hash;
- the claim type and the theorem name;
- the `#print axioms` output for that theorem;
- the verdict.

**Chain.** The chain checker requires `after.sha256` of boundary `i` to equal `before.sha256` of
boundary `i+1`. The final stage must be byte-identical to the file the compilation recipe records as
the `mlir-opt` input. Any gap breaks the chain claim; it is never "mostly verified".

**Replay.** Reruns the checker on the stored bytes; a receipt for different bytes is stale.

### D11. The compiled checker and its trust

The soundness theorem is about the Lean *function*. Running it compiled additionally trusts:

- the Lean compiler and runtime;
- `Lean.Json`;
- the Lean printer used in round trip.

This is the same trust CompCert places in extraction, and it is listed in §10. Forbidding
`implemented_by`/`extern`/`partial`/`unsafe` in checker code (§1.2) is what makes "the compiled
function is the proved function" a compiler-correctness assumption, not a gap. For small instances,
the qualification suite also runs the checker under kernel reduction (`decide`) as a cross-check.

### D12. Coexistence with the SMT layer

- The existing Z3 receipts (`linalg_to_interface`, `interface_to_command_buffer`, `scalar_ir`,
  `outline_ir`, split-K) stay. Where a Lean checker and a Z3 check cover the same boundary, both run,
  and disagreement is a bug in one of them.
- Verdicts use the existing vocabulary in `xdsl_dialects/lowering/passes.py`: `verified`, `refuted`
  (only with an executed counterexample), `abstracted` (rejected or unsupported, with reason), and
  `unmeasured` (not run).
- A new `METHOD_LEAN` joins `filecheck` and `smt`.

## 4. MerlinSem: the Lean semantics

### 4.1 Abstract syntax (frozen at G0)

```lean
inductive Ty
  | int (w : Nat) | index | float (k : FKind) | tensor (shape : List Nat) (elt : Ty) | none
-- static shapes only; a dynamic dimension is `unsupported` at decode time

structure Op where
  name     : String                 -- "linalg.generic", "arith.addi", …
  results  : List (ValueId × Ty)
  operands : List ValueId
  attrs    : List (String × Attr)   -- typed attributes; opaque ones keep printed text
  regions  : List Region
structure Region where blocks : List Block
structure Block  where args : List (ValueId × Ty); ops : List Op
structure Func   where name : String; args : List (ValueId × Ty); body : Block; visibility : Vis
structure Module where funcs : List Func
```

Two attribute kinds need special handling:

- **Semantics-free attributes** (provenance `prov.*`, C-interface markers, contract/schedule
  annotations) are declared in `Spec/Annotations.lean`. That reviewed file is the only place an
  attribute is allowed to be ignored.
- **Every other attribute** is part of an op's identity. An op whose semantics depend on an
  uninterpreted attribute is `unsupported`.

### 4.2 Values

```lean
inductive Scalar | int (w) (v : BitVec w) | idx (v : BitVec ib) | flt (k) (bits : BitVec k.width)
inductive Val    | poison | scalar (s : Scalar)
structure Tensor where shape : List Nat; get : Idx shape → Val   -- functional; Idx = bounded index tuple
inductive Outcome | ub | ok (results : List RVal)               -- RVal = scalar or tensor value
```

`tensor.empty` elements are `poison`. Reading them is permitted, and so is propagating them. §4.5
explains why that is safe and how vacuity is excluded.

### 4.3 Operations

- **Scalar ops** (`arith`, `math`, casts): one total function per op.
  - Integer ops follow the MLIR `arith` spec, including flags.
  - Float ops call the `FloatModel`.
  - `fastmath` flags are part of op identity: a rewrite that changes them is a different op.
- **`linalg.generic`:** the denotation is the loop nest the MLIR spec describes.
  - For each output element, reduction iterators are folded **in lexicographic order of the iteration
    space**, the order `convert-linalg-to-loops` materializes.
  - For integer element types, a proved lemma shows the order is irrelevant.
  - For floats it is the definition. Its fidelity to MLIR is a tested assumption (§9.4).
- **Named linalg ops** (`matmul`, `batch_matmul`, convolutions, `fill`, `transpose`, …) are *defined*
  by their OpDSL generic form, transcribed in `Sem/Named.lean`. Every named-op instance in the paper
  corpus is cross-checked: running `mlir-opt --linalg-generalize-named-ops` on it must produce,
  structurally, the generic form Lean assigns.
- **Tensor movement** (`extract_slice`, `insert_slice`, `collapse_shape`, `expand_shape`, `concat`,
  `pad`, `cast`) is defined by index arithmetic on `Idx`, including rank-reducing slices.
- **Functions and calls.** A program is a module. Calls to defined functions denote their bodies
  (non-recursive; checked). Calls to declarations denote `Γ name`.

Coverage is driven by the WP-0 op census, not by MLIR's full op set. Anything outside the census is
`unsupported` until added (with fidelity tests).

### 4.4 Executable evaluator

`evalArr` is an array-backed interpreter for differential testing (§9.4) and concrete evaluation (C6).

- A theorem `evalArr p x = some y → denote p x = .ok y` connects it to the functional semantics.
- Proving this theorem is part of WP-4. Until it is proved, C6 is not admitted to a verified claim.

### 4.5 Refinement, definedness, and vacuity

```lean
def ValRefines : Val → Val → Prop      -- tgt ⊑ src
  | _,  .poison => True | v, w => v = w
def Refines : Outcome → Outcome → Prop  -- tgt ⊑ src
  | _, .ub => True
  | .ok ys, .ok xs => Forall₂ (RValRefines) ys xs
  | .ub, .ok _ => False
def ProgRefines (D : Domain) (Γok : Env → Prop) (tgt src : Module) : Prop :=
  ∀ (M : FloatModel) (Γ : Env), Γok Γ → ∀ x ∈ D, Refines (denote M Γ tgt x) (denote M Γ src x)
theorem ProgRefines.trans : … -- proved; composition depends on it
```

**The vacuity trap.** If the source reads uninitialized memory into an output, every target refines
it, and the theorem says nothing. The existing `linalg_semantics.py` already refuses an accumulator
initialized from `tensor.empty` for exactly this reason. We handle it two ways:

- **Structural definedness (`Defined₀`).** A checker proves no `poison` from `tensor.empty` or another
  undefined source can reach a function result. This is a dataflow analysis over the IR with a Lean
  soundness proof. It is required of `S0` and of every `policy` input: those are the programs whose
  meaning the paper asserts.
  - Current implementation: `Check/Defined.lean` tracks value types and initialization facts for
    straight-line, single-block programs. Every accepted operation must finish successfully, even
    when its result is not initialized. Every returned value must be initialized. Region arguments
    shadow captures; captures must have justified facts in the actual enclosing environment.
    `Check/DefinedRules.lean` proves rules for scalar and dense tensor constants, ten unflagged
    scalar integer binary operations, legal unflagged `extsi`/`extui`/`trunci` casts,
    `tensor.empty`, `tensor.splat`, and static collapse/expand reshapes.
  - `Check/DefinedLoops.lean` proves total structured-loop execution. Initialized destinations stay
    initialized across reduction steps, including empty iteration spaces. A separate complete-overwrite
    proof allows a dirty destination only when the output map is identity, the loop box equals the
    entire output shape, and the body yields initialized values even with a dirty output argument.
    `Check/DefinedLinalg.lean` checks these conditions against actual `linalg.fill` and `linalg.generic`
    IR. It accepts initialized integer matmul with widening casts, multiple-output reductions,
    captured values, and single-output full overwrites. Structured bodies currently use the leaf
    rulebook; nested structured operations, direct tensor arithmetic,
    division, shifts, and overflow-flagged arithmetic still lack definedness rules.
  - The theorem requires typed, nonpoison inputs. The initialized-matmul fixture passes real xDSL
    preprocessing and obtains a `verified` chain after independent source support/definedness checks.
    This proves that recorded chain, not every model or every matmul lowering. Named matmul and
    batch-matmul also have definedness rules: their proved generic expansions are checked, then a
    separate success-transfer theorem proves successful execution of the actual named source.
  - `linalg.map` now passes the input elements followed by the destination element, matching the
    installed post-LLVM-f5e175f contract. Its progress rule reuses generic safety; its definedness
    rule distinguishes initialized destination reads from complete overwrites of dirty storage.
    Rank-zero/empty tensors and captured values are qualified. The semantic correction is recorded
    in `SPEC_LOCK` and ledger A6. See [the differential guide](../guides/lean_differential_tests.md)
    for compiler provenance, native comparisons, and the separate bufferization-order regression.
  - Typed `linalg.fill` generalization now preserves the source body and checks the scalar broadcast,
    destination identity map and parallel iterators against the checked destination rank. Fill and
    map expansions compose through intermediate SSA values on the same typed domain. Definedness
    remains a separate source obligation; the rewrite itself makes no initializer-content assumption.
- **Support, not just definedness (`NotStuck`).** The semantics returns `stuck` for anything it does
  not model. A per-boundary checker cannot know that the rest of the program is modelled, so each
  boundary claims *conditional* refinement:
  - `RefinesC tgt src := src is stuck ∨ tgt ⊑ src`. It is transitive (proved in
    `Sem/Refine.lean`).
  - The end-to-end claim additionally requires `FuncNotStuck` of the capture `S0` on the whole
    domain. `FuncRefinesC.strict` then turns the chain into strict refinement.
  - This is not a loophole. A non-stuck source is refined only by a non-stuck target, so a boundary
    that introduces an unmodelled construct into a modelled program still fails.
  - `FuncNotStuck(S0)` is established by a support checker. Its soundness rests on a *progress* lemma
    per op family: a supported, well-typed op never returns `stuck`.
  - Implemented in `Check/Progress.lean` (`checkOps_progress`, generic in a per-op typing rule that
    also tracks the loop depth `linalg.index` reads) and `Check/Rules*.lean`. The rules cover
    `arith`/`math` elementwise ops, constants and `select`, `linalg.generic`, `linalg.fill`,
    `linalg.index`, `linalg.transpose`, `tensor.empty`, `tensor.collapse_shape`/`expand_shape`, and
    `cf.assert`. Structured-op accesses are proved in bounds by interval bounds of the indexing maps
    over the iteration box (`Check/Affine.lean`). `Check/Rulebook.lean` assembles the rule and proves
    `supportCheck_sound` and `funcRefines_of_support`, the strict end-to-end form.
- **Value-dependent poison** (an out-of-range `fptosi`, `nsw` overflow) cannot generally be excluded
  without additional input assumptions. The initial definedness checker refuses these operations.
  The planned extension enumerates these sites, and the claim carries them as the explicit
  precondition "for inputs on which these sites are defined".
  - The planned W8A8 extension (`roundeven → clamp → fptosi`) needs a lemma under named
    `FloatLaws` (clamp range on non-NaN) showing the conversion is defined for non-NaN activations.
  - Its domain `D` would then exclude NaN activations, and the claim must say so.

**Current qualification limit:** `supportCheck` establishes `NotStuck`, which permits UB and
poison results. The separate definedness analysis above covers its stated initial fragment;
value-dependent-site enumeration remains unimplemented. A verified support receipt or refinement
claim alone does not establish defined source results. The overall chain verdict requires a
separate source-definedness proof. `Check/DefinedDequant.lean` now supplies that proof for the
locked signed-i8/i32/f32 per-tensor and per-channel source contract on initialized inputs, including
an explicit axis-bounds obligation. Other quantization forms remain unsupported.

### 4.6 Argument and result maps

Some passes change the entry signature: `qinner.lift` appends arguments, entry-weight projection
removes unused ones, prepared transforms reorder them. Their claims are `equiv_args π`, where `π` maps
before-arguments to after-arguments, possibly with "value supplied by tensor T" bindings.

- `π` composes along the chain.
- The runtime ABI checker (C8) uses the composed map to verify the runtime binds each argument to the
  right bytes. So a signature change is verified end to end, not just at the boundary where it
  happened.

## 5. Checkers

Every checker has this shape:

```lean
structure Checker (Cert : Type) where
  claim : ClaimType
  check : Module → Module → Cert → Verdict
  sound : ∀ b a c, check b a c = .verified → claim.Holds b a
```

`claim.Holds` is the D7 meaning. Statements of `sound` live in `Claims/`, are pinned (§8.6), and are
the surface humans review.

### 5.1 C1 — rule-instance checker (local rewrites)

The workhorse, covering most of U1–U15.

**The rule library.** A rule `R` consists of:

- a pattern: a typed subgraph with metavariables for shapes, types, and attributes;
- a replacement;
- side conditions: decidable predicates on the instance;
- a theorem `R.sound`: *for all* metavariable instantiations satisfying the side conditions, the
  replacement's denotation refines (or equals) the pattern's.

`R.sound` is proved **once, parametrically**: for all shapes and widths, and all float models (or
named laws).

**The checker** must:

1. Compute the SSA graph difference between before and after.
2. Partition the changed ops into certificate sites.
3. For each site, check that the before-subgraph matches `R.pattern` under the certified
   instantiation, that the after-subgraph is `R.replacement` under the same instantiation, and that the
   side conditions hold. Side conditions include **purity and no external uses of internal values**,
   which the checker recomputes.
4. Check that everything outside the sites is identical modulo SSA renaming.

**Soundness.** The `sound` proof is `R.sound` lifted by **one congruence theorem**: replacing a
single-entry, single-exit pure subgraph by a refining one refines the program. It is proved once by
induction over the block, with `ProgRefines.trans` for multiple sites.

This is the main lever for parallelism: once the congruence theorem and the rule interface exist, rule
families are independent work.

**Implemented local rewrite families.** DCE, adjacent closed-expression CSE and motion, named
contraction generalization, and unflagged integer operand commutation are proved in `Rules/`.
Generalization checks matmul/batch-matmul indexing maps and iterator order and preserves the original region body,
including signed/unsigned casts and floating operation order. Integer commutation covers add,
multiply, AND, OR, and XOR, with scalar and tensor semantics; floats and overflow flags are refused.
Qualification runs actual MLIR generalization/canonicalization/CSE, verifies defined chains on the
supported integer fragment, and rejects altered maps, body operations, casts, constants, and flags.
Operand-dependent/non-adjacent CSE and other numerical or shape rewrites remain unsupported.
Map-to-generic certification now has a separate typed route. `TypedRewrite` proves normalization
using runtime facts derived from checked source execution, including intermediate SSA values.
`MapGeneralize` proves identity maps, operand segmentation, iterator order and unchanged bodies
at the justified destination rank. `TypedC1` composes that normalization with the existing C1
script on the actual target. The universal C1 theorem remains unchanged; typed claims explicitly
require the declared argument types and index width. `Motion` separately proves adjacent swaps
of two pure, region-free, operand-free, single-result expressions. It checks distinct source
result ids and uses alpha simulation in target order to preserve both bindings and escaping
uses. The synthesizer proposes a two-operation site; C1 independently checks it. This covers
upstream hoisting a captured constant across a static `tensor.empty`, including composition
with typed map expansion. Operand-taking or region-bearing motion, non-adjacent hoisting and
nested rewrites remain `abstracted` without additional proofs or synthesis.
The untrusted script synthesizer cannot bypass these checks.

**Rule families for Tier A** (exact list fixed by WP-0):

- dead pure-op elimination;
- `quant_ext.dequantize_per_channel` → `linalg.generic`;
- TorchAO affine quantize/dequantize lowering;
- over-rank matmul collapse;
- bool `sitofp`/`fptosi` fixes (frontend repairs: signed `i1` true means −1, whereas the intended
  boolean-to-float value is +1; float-to-bool means nonzero, not an integer truncation);
  these need independently declared frontend semantics rather than ordinary MLIR equivalence;
- scalar-square canonicalization (`powf(x, 2)` to `mulf(x, x)` needs an independently justified
  floating-point law; it is not provable for the current arbitrary `FloatModel`);
- quant round/clamp/convert fusion;
- capture-normalization transforms;
- accumulator zero-fill (claim `refine`);
- the post-offload feature rewrites that WP-0 finds enabled.

### 5.1.1 Ordered upstream rewrite implementation

Implement one independently qualified family at a time. Each family needs a soundness theorem,
acceptance of actual pass output, independently corrupted negative outputs, source-definedness
coverage for its demonstration, receipt replay, and a separate commit. Compilation below the
model/tensor layer is outside this sequence.

| Order | Family | Proof obligation and completion criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Named operation generalization | Matmul/batch-matmul use universal expansion; map/fill/copy/transpose/broadcast/reduce use checked typed SSA facts. Implemented for supported top-level forms, including variadic reduction. Source-definedness, actual pass output and defined chains are checked separately. Identity folds and other named families remain outstanding. |
| 2 | Exact integer arithmetic | Operand commutation and typed one-operation normalization are implemented. Next extend typed sites to width/flag-aware multi-operation cast and constant simplifications. Check signedness, poison and actual canonicalizer output. |
| 3 | Valid static shape rewrites | Prove reshape cancellation/composition and indexing equivalence, including zero extents, reassociation, element type, and all escaping uses. |
| 4 | Rewrites inside operation bodies | Lift proved local rules into actual linalg region closures with captures and block-argument shadowing; preserve reduction order and unchanged computation. |
| 5 | Exact quantization expansion | Check dequantization and already-declared quantization operations against independent software semantics. Preserve broadcast/granularity maps, zero points, widths, rounding, clamps, and floating operation order. |
| 6 | Numerical policies and frontend repairs | Add policy-specific claims for dynamic W8A8/approximations and frontend-intent claims for capture repairs. Compose only after independent policy review and qualification on complete upstream runs. |

`Rules/OneOp.lean` provides a reusable expansion contract: preserve operands, result bindings,
and actual region closures; prove the candidate agrees on every modeled, defined source execution.
Its soundness theorem lifts this contract through the existing SSA/region correspondence and C1
interface. A constructor is not proof evidence: semantic checks decode its proposed operation again.
Named generalization has a separate success-transfer theorem so source-definedness cannot be inferred
from a target that merely repairs an undefined or unsupported source.

**Typed local obligations.** The current C1 site interface quantifies over every runtime environment.
Type annotations alone do not establish runtime operand widths or canonical tensor representation.
Cast/shape elimination rules must carry proved local typing/canonicality facts under an explicit
input domain, or establish correctness without those assumptions. Do not weaken the existing
universal theorem or assume well-typed operands merely because an IR annotation says so.

Implemented foundation: `Check/TypedRewrite.lean` provides a `TypedExpansion` contract and proves
that successful source steps establish the types of their actual results. Normalization preserves
operands, result bindings and region closures. `TypedC1.check_sound` and `TypedModule.check_sound`
are separately pinned claims on typed inputs at one index width. The first qualified expansion
is map-to-generic; fill and copy generalization use the same interface. Copy retains the actual
body and proves both conditional refinement and transfer of successful generic execution back
to the original named source. Its definedness rule independently checks full overwrite or an
initialized destination, so copying poison never becomes a defined-source claim.

`Rules/LayoutGeneralize.lean` shares layout reconstruction, generic header decoding, typed
expansion and successful-source transfer for transpose, broadcast and variadic reduce. Transpose
uses the inverse permutation for its input map; broadcast projects away inserted dimensions;
reduce keeps the original input/output ordering, projected output maps and reduction iterators.
No scalar body or floating arithmetic order changes. Reduction operand partitioning matches the
existing semantics, including the implicit equal-half split exported by upstream MLIR. A separate
support rule proves progress for that variadic reduction representation. The definedness checker
reuses generic totality and complete-overwrite proofs, then transfers success to the actual named
source. Initialized integer reductions, captured scalar inputs and multiple results are covered;
floating reduction refinement is available, but its arithmetic definedness remains unsupported by
the current leaf rulebook. Identity operations folded away by upstream greedy rewriting require
a separate rule and remain explicit abstentions. Rank-zero transpose cannot be qualified with the
current native tools: upstream verification attempts to construct an empty permutation map and
crashes. This is retained as an upstream discrepancy, not counted as a passing comparison.

This one-operation interface does not yet implement multi-operation cast
fusion, reshape elimination, nested rewriting or numerical policies.

**Uniform input compaction.** `Rules/StructuredInputs.lean` proves that two independently checked
structured layouts execute identically when their loop ranges, output maps and body input values
agree. It retains the actual body, captures, output tensors and lexicographic reduction order.
`Rules/SplatInputs.lean` instantiates this theorem for full uniform tensors replaced by rank-zero
uniform tensors and empty indexing maps. Uniformity follows from construction with the same scalar
payload, including poison; a tensor type alone supplies no uniformity fact. The layout checker
rejects a lost or changed loop extent, invalid access and unsupported affine geometry.
`Check/BindingFacts.lean` supplies a general dependency-aware fact domain: rebinding an identifier
invalidates every fact depending on it. `Check/UniformFacts.lean` learns a tensor's precise scalar
producer from successful splat execution, proves retained facts valid after a whole source prefix,
and couples source/target payloads only through actual corresponding scalar bindings. It also
proves totality of splat construction from an actually bound scalar. These facts preserve poison
and do not follow from tensor types. Mutation checks cover payload/tensor shadowing, self-shadowing
producers, unrelated writes, unsupported attributes, malformed producers and dynamic/encoded types.
These are proved components, not yet a whole-program SSA rewrite checker or a verified pass
receipt. Checking the actual rewritten generic headers, result correspondence, all escaping SSA
uses and region closures remains necessary. Native differential tests execute real `scalarize_splat_inputs` output,
including multiple splats, retained full-tensor results, affine indexing, empty/rank-zero tensors
and float bit copies, with a corrupted-payload negative and a separate integer reference.

Over-rank matmul capture repair is **not** an ordinary shape equivalence obligation. Its source is
explicitly invalid linalg (`collapse_overrank_matmul`), so proving refinement against its unsupported
MLIR meaning is insufficient. It belongs with independently declared frontend semantics in order 6.
Boolean cast repairs have the same distinction. Quantization policy authors must use independently
declared software contracts/references rather than reconstruct the policy from the implementing pass.
**First arithmetic-lowering obligation.** `Spec/Dequant.lean` proposes separate scalar contracts
for signed-i8 input, i32 zero point and f32 scale/result, based on PyTorch v2.10.0. Per-tensor
dequantization converts before subtraction; per-channel dequantization subtracts after integer
promotion and converts once. `Rules/DequantScalar.lean` proves execution of each ordered scalar
body against its contract for every float model and scalar input bit pattern, including all
combinations of poison arithmetic operands and an arbitrary unused destination value.
`checkBody_poison_sound` lifts that result through the proved SSA alpha
checker. Tests compare the actual MLIR-exported per-tensor lowering body, reject the current
per-channel float-subtraction body, and accept the proposed integer-subtraction replacement.
`Rules/DequantTensor.lean` lifts accepted bodies to regions closed over arbitrary SSA environments
and entire generic loop nests, including empty iteration spaces. Its `checkLayout` checks the
source-derived identity/broadcast/channel-projection maps, parameter shapes, axis, iterator kinds,
and interval-proved access bounds. `tensor_contract_sound` then proves that the complete generic
loop computes the independently specified `Spec.Dequant.tensor`: q uses the output coordinate,
parameters use either the empty coordinate or the selected channel coordinate. It derives the
pointwise reads, covers arbitrary ranks and poison elements, and permits any destination values.
The narrow `Sem/Ops/Dequant.lean` source family now checks runtime shapes and observable element
values, and evaluates that independent source contract. `Check/RulesDequant.lean` proves source
support on typed inputs; this prevents the claim from depending on an unsupported source.
`Rules/DequantSource.lean` connects actual runtime tensors to the loop proof. The C1 rule in
`Rules/DequantSite.lean` checks source/target operation structure, every operand mapping, output
and parameter shapes, attributes, fresh result/temporary names, maps, iterators and the scalar body.
Its soundness proof preserves SSA correspondence across the target-only allocation. `Synth` offers
this one-to-two site to C1; the existing C1 and module checkers recheck it. Unsupported variants
remain refused. This source-family addition is the frozen-dispatch interface extension requested
by the narrow-lowering verification work; the dispatcher change and family are pinned in SPEC_LOCK.
Source-contract review and capture-producer binding remain necessary for paper qualification.
The production signed-i8/i32/f32 per-channel lowering now emits the proved integer-subtraction
body. Both narrow granularities verify on actual pass output. The historical floating-subtraction
per-channel body is retained as a refused negative control, including its large-zero-point numeric
counterexample. Other datatype and precision combinations remain outside this lowering proof.

**Narrow whole-pass milestone.** Authoritative tests now export real preprocessing boundaries
with the full signed-i8 bounds and `input_dtype = "i8"` metadata, check every stage and source
support, bind the upstream handoff, and replay the receipts into a strict `chain_refines` claim
reported as `refinement_verdict`. A separate dequantization definedness proof establishes successful,
nonpoison source results on initialized inputs for every parametric float model. Replaying its
receipt gives an overall verified chain for this narrow contract; omitting that receipt retains an
abstracted overall verdict. Definedness does not establish IEEE fidelity or producer binding.
Changing emitted subtraction to addition after verification invalidates the chain. Additional
checks cover multiple dequantization sites, reversed returned-result order, rank-zero tensors,
empty tensors and nonzero channel axes. This is verification relative to the proposed source
model (T3), with nonnegative axes, rank-zero per-tensor parameters, and omitted or full-range
signed-i8 bounds; it is not yet paper-qualified capture-to-execution verification.

The separate CPU source oracle in `lean-torch-requirements.txt` pins PyTorch 2.10.0+cpu by wheel
hash and all installed package versions. `test_lean_dequant_torch.py` executes the actual source
operations for 63 finite-input tensor cases (539 element results), including signed zero, both i32
wraparound directions and the historical large-zero-point counterexample. It compares every result
bit with independent NumPy arithmetic and 49 selected points with actual compiler bodies folded
by authoritative MLIR. Set `MERLIN_TORCH_PYTHON` to that isolated interpreter; required-mode runs
fail if it is absent. These bounded fidelity checks supplement the universal parametric proofs;
NaN payload and complete IEEE-instance qualification, human specification review, and exact
capture-producer binding remain outstanding. CI is configured to provision the separate oracle;
commits made with `[skip ci]` are validated locally.

### 5.2 C2 — outlining (inline-and-compare)

A generalization of `verify/outline_ir.py`, with these steps:

1. Inline every call from the driver to a private, non-recursive, defined `$kernel_` function.
2. Dead-code-eliminate cloned producers that the outliner leaves in the driver (it does so by design).
3. Require the result to be isomorphic to the source modulo SSA renaming.

**Theorem:** inlining a pure non-recursive defined function preserves denotation. DCE is C1's
dead-op rule.

**Domain.** It extends the Python checker to `tensor.empty`/`linalg.fill` clones, `linalg.generic`
regions, and region-captured values lifted to operands. These are the cases `outline_ir.py` currently
abstains on.

### 5.3 C3 — erasure and serialization

- **`erasure`:** strip the attributes and ops that `Spec/Annotations.lean` declares semantics-free,
  then require isomorphism. Covers `add_c_interface`, provenance stripping, contract and schedule
  annotations.
- **Serialization (U16):** the dual-export isomorphism of D9.

### 5.4 C4 — partition

`form_groups` changes no IR. Its output is a group table. C4 proves the table is a **partition** of
the computational ops of `@forward`: every op is in exactly one group, and each group is closed under
the declared consumer-closure rule. It cross-checks the group table against the outliner's kernels
(group *i* ↔ kernel *i*).

The claim is structural. It establishes the denominator that makes "every eligible group was routed
or justified on the host" checkable, and it feeds `group_offload.require_every_group_accounted`.

### 5.5 C5 — numerical policy implementation

**Policy as a spec transformer.** A policy is a **spec transformer** `P : Module → Module` written in
`Spec/Policy/*.lean`. The W8A8 transformer replaces each eligible contraction by:

1. symmetric per-row dynamic activation quantization, with
   `s = max(max|x|/127, 1e-5)` and `q = fptosi(roundeven(x · (1/s)))`, clamped;
2. `i8×i8→i32` accumulation;
3. requantization `acc · ∏ scales`, in the exact operation order the declared policy states.

**Information barrier.** The spec author works from the declared policy (the quantization recipe, the
TorchAO documentation and reference code), not from `passes_quant_int.py`.

**The checker.** C5 is C1 with rules whose right-hand side is the *policy expansion* rather than an
equivalent rewrite. The theorem is `after ⊑ P(before)`.

**Side theorems** (reported in the paper, checked per contraction):

- **No i32 accumulator overflow:** `K · max|a| · max|b| < 2³¹` from the operand types, so integer
  accumulation is exact over ℤ.
- **Clamp-before-convert definedness** for non-NaN inputs (§4.5).

**Why the claim relates `Sn` to `P(Sk)` and not to `P(S0)`.** A syntactic policy transformer is not
monotone under refinement: `Sk ⊑ S0` does not imply `P(Sk) ⊑ P(S0)`. So the composed claim (§5.10)
names `Sk` explicitly, and §1.1 states `Sk ⊑ S0` separately. The chain makes `Sk` exactly the
program the policy was applied to.

**Agreement with TorchAO** (the comment "matching TorchAO's captured arithmetic") is an *empirical*
claim. It is checked by differential execution against eager PyTorch with the same policy, not by
this proof.

### 5.6 C6 — constant evaluation (quant hoist, prepack values)

`quant_hoist` replaces a run-time quantize chain over a weight argument by a precomputed constant.
C6 checks each such site like this:

1. It recomputes the chain on the actual weight bytes, using `evalArr` with the **executable IEEE
   `FloatModel`**.
2. It requires bit equality with the constant.
3. It requires that the replaced subgraph depends only on that weight argument, which is immutable by
   the runtime ABI (C8).

The theorem is conditional on the IEEE instance (D5), and the claim names that assumption.

### 5.7 C7 — the device seam

- **C7a — routing rewrite.** On the paper path (BY_GROUP, §2.1) the rewrite replaces a group's ops
  *directly* with `call @<stem>_N(live-ins)` and adds a private declaration. No outlined `$kernel_i`
  function is created.
  - The certificate names the group's ops.
  - The checker re-extracts that subgraph from the before-program and checks that it is closed: its
    live-ins and live-outs are exactly the call's operands and results.
  - It defines the contract `Γ @<stem>_N := ⟦extracted group⟧`, a function from live-ins to
    live-outs.
  - **Theorem:** under any `Γ` meeting those contracts, the programs are equivalent. It follows from
    congruence (C1) with the call as the replacement site.
  - A device sidecar entry must exist for every new symbol, and vice versa.
  - The subsequent text patch of declaration argument attributes is checked as an `erasure`-class
    boundary (C3). Its attributes must be on the reviewed semantics-free list, or the boundary is
    `abstracted`.
  - The older route that outlines first (C2, then a call) is not on the paper path.
- **C7b — entry fidelity.** `group_command.program` restates group *i* as a generator entry
  (contraction extents, stored operand, bias axis, epilogue, `acc_scale`, convolution geometry, output
  type).
  - `Spec/Entry.lean` gives the entry vocabulary a denotation `EntrySpec : Entry → TensorFn`, written
    behind the information barrier from the corpus/capsule vocabulary documentation.
  - C7b proves `⟦kernel_i⟧ = unlay ∘ EntrySpec(entry_i) ∘ lay`, where `lay`/`unlay` are the declared
    restatements (weight-stationary orientation, patch formation, bias axis).
  - Checking is per instance, by normal-form comparison of the group's contraction + epilogue chain
    against the spec. This is the narrow, structured case of C11.
  - This is the check that ensures the OOT is asked to implement the right function.
- **C7c — ABI bridge (below tensor level).** This covers the generated C shim and the prepack.
  - **Layout lemma:** the declared kernel layout (row-major, edge tiles zero-padded to the tile edge)
    is a bijection on in-range indices, and padding positions do not affect in-range outputs given the
    kernel contract.
  - **Prepack check:** recompute the prepacked stored tensor from the weight bytes and require byte
    equality.
  - **Shim conformance:** check structurally that each generated shim instance equals the reviewed
    template, instantiated with parameters derived from the call signature.
  - **Contiguity at the call site:** prove that every memref passed to a device call is contiguous
    row-major. This comes from the static layout in the bufferized IR, or from a run-time guard the
    shim already emits. Otherwise the boundary is `abstracted`.

### 5.8 C8 — runtime ABI binding

`c_runtime.generate` emits `model_gen.h` (the argument table), `model_call.c`, `weights.bin` and
`model_io.h`. C8 consumes:

- a JSON twin of the table, emitted by the generator and checked against the header by deterministic
  re-rendering;
- the exported entry signature;
- the safetensors manifest;
- the composed argument map `π` (§4.6).

It proves, per argument:

- kind, rank, dims and element size match the entry's type;
- every weight offset range lies inside `weights.bin` and is pairwise disjoint;
- the bytes at that range equal the safetensors tensor bound to that argument by `π`;
- state-carry entries pair a program's state output with the same-typed state input of the next
  program in the session.

The C runtime that turns the table into memref descriptors (`merlin/runtime/c/merlin_model.c`) is
small, reviewed, unit-tested and listed in the TCB.

### 5.9 C9 — memory map and allocation safety

- **C9a — link layout.** From the linked ELF's section headers and the layout record (arena, weights,
  stack reserve, code reserve, static IO), prove pairwise disjointness and containment in the board's
  DRAM. This is a Lean interval checker; reading the ELF is trusted tooling.
- **Static stack bound (Tier B).** Combine per-function frame sizes (`-fstack-usage`) with the call
  graph from LLVM IR. Requires no recursion and no dynamic `alloca`. This closes the failure that
  `spike_model.build` documents (stack overflow silently corrupting `.bss`/HTIF).
- **C9b — arena binding, only if WP-0 finds `static_arena` on the paper path.** Re-derive each
  allocation's live interval independently of `arena_plan`, then check:
  - the allocation runs at most once per call;
  - `malloc` dominates and `free` post-dominates;
  - the pointer does not escape;
  - the planned offsets are in bounds, aligned, and disjoint for overlapping intervals.

  The theorem relates the arena program to the `malloc` program on an abstract block memory model
  (memory injection). This checker is **target-agnostic by construction**: capacity and alignment are
  parameters. After submission it is the same checker Agustin asked for, applied to OOT local storage.

### 5.10 C10 — chain and composition

`Claims/Compose.lean` states the per-program theorem of §1.1 as a function of the ordered boundary
verdicts and proves it from `ProgRefines.trans`, the claim types, and the argument-map algebra.

The chain checker:

1. Verifies hash continuity.
2. Verifies that each receipt replays.
3. Instantiates the theorem.
4. Emits the claim with its explicit lists of assumed sites, trusted boundaries, named float laws, and
   value-dependent poison preconditions.

Implemented for refinement chains: `Check/Chain.lean` proves `chain_refinesC` (a chain of
`FuncRefinesC` boundaries composes) and `chain_refines` (with the capture's support, strict
refinement on typed inputs). `merlin.verify.lean.check.compose_chain` checks hash continuity from
the support subject through every boundary, validates receipt metadata and provenance, and replays
the round trips and Lean checks on the exact recorded exports. It writes a `merlin.lean.chain.v1`
document with the union of assumptions and a separate `refinement_verdict`. This proves refinement
under the recorded assumptions; source UB and poison are still permitted by that relation.
`check_definedness` writes a separate `defined` receipt for the initial fragment from §4.5.
Passing its path as `compose_chain(..., definedness=...)` requires replay of that proof on the same
source, function, and index width. `chain_defined_refines` then establishes refinement and successful,
nonpoison target execution on typed, nonpoison inputs. Without that receipt, or outside its fragment,
the overall verdict remains `abstracted`. Replay also binds the actual stage files to the recorded byte references, so a receipt cannot
retarget a live stage to another file. Earlier receipts, live stage files, sources, and the handoff
are checked again before publication. Argument maps (`equiv_args`) and numerical policies are not composed yet.
This claim covers the recorded chain, not the entire compilation pipeline. No caller-provided
definedness flag can replace the proof.

Typed boundaries use `check_typed_stage(..., index_bits=...)`, with the independent
`TypedModule.check_sound` theorem. Mixed universal/typed chains use `chain_typed_refines` and
`chain_typed_defined_refines` from `Check/TypedChain.lean`. Replay compares the bound function
argument types, including tensor shapes, across every link and requires typed index widths to
match the source receipts. The original chain theorems and legacy receipt behavior are unchanged.

### 5.11 C11 — upstream tensor passes (Tier B) and bufferization (Tier C)

**C11 (Tier B).** Upstream `canonicalize`/`cse`/`linalg-fuse-elementwise-ops`/
`linalg-generalize-named-ops` emit no certificates. C11 validates them by **normal-form comparison**:

- Each output is denoted symbolically as an element expression over *symbolic output indices*:
  quasi-affine index terms, folds for reductions, and piecewise guards for slices, pads and concats.
- The expressions are hash-consed and normalized: affine and `floordiv`/`mod`-by-constant
  normalization, alpha-renaming of bound reduction variables, and structural equality, because floats
  are parametric.
- Its theorem is that normalization preserves denotation. Incompleteness yields `abstracted`, never a
  false accept.
- Enumerating concrete output indices is *not* used: a TinyLlama projection would be ~10⁹ terms.

**Tier C.** Bufferization needs a memref semantics with aliasing. It is planned as a symbolic-store
checker over static layouts and is out of scope before submission.

## 6. The frontend boundary (PyTorch → linalg)

PyTorch has no formal semantics, so a theorem here needs a **reference specification** of the ATen
operators used. The principled source is PyTorch's own executable reference implementations
(`torch._refs`, written in terms of `prims`), which exist to be a specification.

Tier B plan:

1. Transcribe the ATen-core subset in the WP-0 census into `Spec/ATen/*.lean` behind the information
   barrier, following `_refs` operation order.
2. Validate each spec differentially against eager PyTorch.
3. Check each FX node's MLIR region against its spec, using the trace's node ↔ MLIR correspondence
   (`targetgen/frontend_trace.py` already validates `m2m.frontend_graph.v1` and its correspondence
   maps).

**Prerequisite (external).** The frontend graph snapshot must carry every node's full arguments
(scalars, dims, eps, strides), not only target, shape and dtype. If model2MLIR's exporter does not,
that is a change in the model2MLIR repository.

**Without Tier B,** U0 is reported as *differential evidence only*: capture attestation plus eager
PyTorch vs MLIR execution on the recorded inputs. The paper says exactly that.

## 7. Below the tensor level

What remains after `Sn`:

- **Bufferization, MLIR → LLVM, clang, ld:** trusted, with Tier C as the path to shrink it. Upstream
  vectorization schedules may reassociate float reductions; that is part of the trusted lowering's
  numerical behavior. Integer contractions are unaffected (D6).
- **Merlin LLVM-IR transforms (D3), if on the paper path:**
  - preferred: `alive-tv` per function pair (method `alive2`, bounded TV of LLVM IR), recorded as
    non-Lean evidence;
  - otherwise listed as assumed;
  - arena binding is C9b.
- **Device ABI, runtime binding, memory map:** C7c, C8, C9.
- **End-to-end execution:** the existing complete-output check (`verified_complete_output` in
  `baremetal_model.py`) and L3 grading remain the evidence that the trusted segments and the contract
  assumptions hold on the inputs run. That is evidence, not proof, and is labelled as such.

## 8. Integration with Merlin

### 8.1 Stage capture

Every boundary on the WP-0 inventory gets a recorded `(before, after, certificate)` triple.

- The xDSL preprocessing already records generic-form stages (`ir_inspection.record_stage`).
- WP-7 extends recording to `zephyr_model._prepare_model_mlir` and `prepare_for_lowering` (quant
  passes, lift, hoist, routing, feature rewrites) and to `lower_model`'s upstream boundary.
- Recording uses `ir_audit` exact mode and writes under `out/artifacts/verification/`.

### 8.2 Certificate recorder

`src/merlin/verify/lean/certify.py` exposes `record(rule, site, params)`. It is a no-op when
recording is off. Passes call it at their existing rewrite points.

- **Byte-neutrality** is a test: build a fixed corpus with recording on and off, and require digests to
  be identical for every IR stage, `model.o` and the ELF.

### 8.3 Receipts, verdicts, CLI

- `merlin-verify lean check --boundary <id> --before … --after … --cert …` runs the export, round
  trip and checker, and writes a `merlin.lean.receipt.v1`.
- `merlin-verify lean chain <build-dir>` runs every boundary of a build and C10.
- `merlin-verify lean replay <receipt>` re-checks stored bytes.
- Verdicts flow through `record_verification(..., method=METHOD_LEAN)` into the shared log that
  `check_pass_obligations.py` reads.

### 8.4 Coverage matrix

`merlin-verify lean coverage` renders, per program and per boundary (from the WP-0 denominator):

- claim type;
- verdict;
- sites verified, assumed and unsupported, with reasons;
- method;
- TCB membership.

This is the paper's verification table and is generated, never hand-edited, consistent with
`agentic_experiment_integrity.md`.

### 8.5 Failure handling

**Debugging aid.** A rejected boundary gets a *counterexample search*: run `evalArr` on random and
boundary inputs, with the IEEE instance, on both sides.

- A confirmed difference is `refuted` and can become a capsule through `verify/counterexamples.py`.
- An unconfirmed rejection stays `abstracted`.

**Findings ledger.** Real Merlin bugs found this way are fixed in separate commits with regression
tests and logged in a findings ledger. They are paper material.

### 8.6 Guardrails (CI and pre-commit)

- **`lean-guard`:**
  - scans declarations in the verified namespaces and declarations defined in their modules for the
    §1.2 ban list, including helpers in other namespaces; compiler-generated recursive twins are
    exempt only from the unsafe/partial checks, and assumption axioms must originate in locked
    `MerlinVerify.Assumptions` modules;
  - runs `#print axioms` on every theorem in `Claims/` and compares it to the allow-list. On
    v4.35.0, `bv_decide` adds a per-proof native axiom (`<thm>._native.bv_decide.ax_…`), so this audit
    catches it without a separate tactic ban. Fixed-width lemmas use `decide`/`omega` or are listed in
    `Assumptions/`;
  - hashes the elaborated *statement* of every `Claims/` theorem and every `Spec/` definition against
    `merlin/lean/SPEC_LOCK`. Any change fails unless `SPEC_LOCK` is updated in a reviewed commit.
- **Build and tests:** `lake build` with Mathlib cache, Lean unit tests (`#guard` on golden JSON),
  Python tests, and the byte-neutrality test.
- **Workflow:** a new `lean.yml` alongside `pr-fast.yml`.

## 9. Qualification: evidence that the verifier works

A checker that has never rejected anything is unqualified. Required before any paper number is
frozen:

1. **Seeded faults (mutation testing).** Per rule family, mutation operators applied to *real* pass
   output must all be rejected, with a matrix in the style of `verify/evaluate.py`:
   - swap operands;
   - change an indexing map;
   - drop or duplicate an epilogue stage;
   - perturb a constant or scale;
   - change a reduction to parallel;
   - mis-wire a call operand;
   - remove an accumulator fill;
   - break an argument map;
   - shift a weight offset;
   - overlap two live intervals.
2. **Non-vacuity.** Every checker accepts the real paper-pipeline output for every program it covers,
   and `Defined₀` holds for `S0` and every policy input.
3. **Historical bug replay.** Use the `merlin-analysis` replay methodology:
   - fix the population first (every `fix(` commit touching a verified pass);
   - draw with a recorded seed;
   - report how many pre-fix outputs the checker rejects;
   - report unreplayable commits explicitly.

   This is the strongest evidence that the verification would have mattered.
4. **Semantics fidelity** (an independent agent, not the semantics author):
   - differential execution of `evalArr` against `mlir-runner` on every op form in the census, with
     integer results exact and float results bitwise under matching operation order;
   - the named-op generalization cross-check (§4.3);
   - the exhaustive and boundary IEEE-instance tests (D5).
5. **Spec review.** Every `Spec/` file is reviewed by a human (Jeremy or Agustin) against its cited
   source (MLIR op docs, TorchAO, `torch._refs`), and the review is recorded in `SPEC_LOCK`.
6. **Axioms and ban audit**, and receipt replay from a clean checkout on a second machine.

**Executable MLIR integration tests.** `.github/workflows/lean-mlir.yml` builds and audits Lean,
then requires the pinned compiler Python MLIR bindings for round trips, receipts, chain replay,
and the receipt-forgery and file-race regressions. `test_lean_mlir_differential.py` compares concrete
integer results with MLIR's constant folder at widths 1, 2, 8, 32 and 64, including boundary values,
division, shifts and overflow flags. Immediate UB is classified independently from the generated
inputs; a Lean `ub` answer alone never exempts a case from comparison. The JUnit results record
constant, poison, UB and unfolded counts, and require measured coverage for every tested operation.
Legal folds from poison to concrete values are counted separately from exact-value agreement.
Named map, reduce, matmul and transpose also compare with MLIR's generalization and independently
specified expected tensors, including nonzero destination initializers. These are bounded tests,
not a proof of MLIR fidelity. Full `mlir-runner` census coverage and IEEE-instance testing remain
qualification work; the concrete Lean test driver deliberately supplies no IEEE float oracle.

To reproduce the authoritative tests on Linux x86_64 with Python 3.12 and `uv` installed:

```sh
uv sync --frozen --extra dev --extra verify --extra targetgen
uv venv --python 3.12 out/build/lean/mlir-venv
export MERLIN_M2M_PYTHON="$PWD/out/build/lean/mlir-venv/bin/python"
uv pip install --python "$MERLIN_M2M_PYTHON" --no-deps -r build_tools/toolchains/lean-mlir-requirements.txt
# Install elan, then fetch Mathlib's cache in merlin/lean: lake exe cache get
export PATH="$HOME/.elan/bin:$PATH"
uv run --no-sync python build_tools/scripts/check_lean_guard.py
MERLIN_LEAN_REQUIRED=1 uv run --no-sync pytest -q merlin/tests/ir/test_lean*.py \
  -k 'not standalone and not test_round_trip_identical' \
  -o junit_family=xunit1 \
  --junitxml=out/artifacts/verification/lean-mlir.xml
```

The isolated binding environment uses the same torch-mlir version as
`build_tools/toolchains/compiler-python.toml`, with a checksum-bound wheel. It needs no model capture
checkout or PyTorch frontend. `MERLIN_COMPILER_PYTHON` also selects the test interpreter when the
compatibility variable `MERLIN_M2M_PYTHON` is absent. Standalone xDSL/`mlir-opt` comparisons remain
available separately through `MERLIN_VERIFY_MLIR_OPT`; they do not substitute for the authoritative
tests in this workflow.

**What would make this verification fake, and the guard against each:**

| Failure mode | Guard |
|---|---|
| Checker validates bytes other than those compiled | Byte-bound receipts, chain continuity, final-stage identity with the recipe's `mlir-opt` input (D10) |
| Exporter misreads IR | MLIR's own parser plus round trip (D9) |
| Lean semantics differ from MLIR | Differential fidelity tests and named-op cross-check (§9.4) |
| Spec copies the implementation's bug | Information barrier and human spec review (§1.2.6, §9.5) |
| Proof of the wrong theorem | Pinned, reviewed statements in `Claims/` (§8.6) |
| `sorry`, axioms, or implementation swap | Ban list, axioms audit, no `implemented_by` (§1.2.1) |
| Vacuous refinement | `Defined₀` plus explicit value-dependent preconditions (§4.5) |
| Silent scope narrowing | Pre-registered denominator, all-or-nothing boundary verdicts, coverage matrix |
| Numerics change reported as equivalence | Claim types (D7) |
| Instrumentation changes the compiler | Byte-neutrality test (§8.2) |

  change requests and `SPEC_LOCK` updates. Jeremy and Agustin review every `Spec/` and `Claims/`
  statement. Those files are the entire human-reading burden, and they are kept small on purpose.
- **Rules every agent brief must include:**
  - the §1.2 list, verbatim;
  - "do not change the pass you verify, except to add recorder calls; a suspected pass bug goes to
    the findings ledger";
  - "an unsupported construct is reported, never skipped";
  - "do not weaken or delete a test; WP-RT reviews test diffs";
  - "write the soundness statement before the checker".

### 11.5 Gates, schedule and scope tiers

**Rigor is fixed; coverage is what flexes.** If time runs short, a boundary moves from verified to
*assumed* or *trusted*, explicitly. The checks never get weaker.

| Gate | Content | Target |
|---|---|---|
| G0 | Interfaces frozen; inventory started | day 3 |
| G1 | Walking skeleton, with all guards enforced | end of week 1–1.5 |
| G2 | First full chain for the smallest validation program | end of week 3–4 |
| G3 | **Tier A** for every validation program: every boundary the measured inventory puts on the paper path (per §2.1 currently U2–U5, U9, the added preparation rows, U12 and its declaration patch, U15 and the `ciface_inline` LLVM transform), plus C7b–c, C8, C9a and C10; qualification complete | end of week 5–6 |
| G4 | Evidence frozen: coverage matrix, ledger, replay on a clean machine | G3 + ~1 week |

- **Tier B** (C11 upstream tensor passes, frontend ATen specs, stack bound) runs in parallel from G1
  and lands if ready by G4.
- **Tier C** (bufferization, Alive2 integration, float error bounds, session semantics) is
  post-submission.

These estimates assume eight to ten concurrent agents and a working model2MLIR environment by day 3.
The paper deadline (§13) decides how much of Tier B is attempted.

## 12. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Core semantics or congruence proof takes longer than planned (critical path) | Freeze simple representations (functional tensors, extrinsic AST); start WP-6 on the scalar fragment while movement ops land; rules proceed against the frozen `denote` signature |
| model2MLIR environment or checkpoints unavailable | Run WP-0 on public model capsules first; the paper models are needed only by G2 |
| Paper path uses many optional feature rewrites | WP-0 fixes the list early; each feature is a rule family, which parallelizes; un-certified features become explicitly assumed sites |
| Exporter cannot be built against the pinned MLIR | Python-bindings fallback (D9); never fall back to trusting xDSL's parser alone |
| Float rewrites in Merlin passes are not exact-order | They are then `policy` or rejected. A finding is a result, not a reason to add a float law. |
| Lean or Mathlib version churn | Pin once at G0 and do not upgrade before submission |
| Agents game the checks | §1.2 guardrails in CI, WP-RT veto, pinned statements, mutation matrices |
| Checker runtime on large programs | Linear algorithms only (C11 symbolic, never enumerative); compiled checker; measure at G2 |
